1. What Recall Score is. 
Open  a file
play it
slow it down and speed it up
arrow through some notes 

Open a score and arrow through a few bars while notes sound and NVDA reads them. This is the   
hook, so keep it under a minute.                                                                                        
2. Getting around. The five regions, Tab and Z/X/C/V/B to jump between them, and moving by beat, by bar and by jump     
point.                                                                                                                  
3. Learning a part. Solo or mute parts, filter to one staff or voice, then loop a passage.                              
4. Playback. Play, pause, the lead-in, the position announcer and the metronome.                                        
5. Reading the detail. What the attributes region tells you about selected notes, and hiding attributes you don't need. 
6. Performance markings. Dynamics, hairpins, repeats, codas and al capo, as found in region 5.                          
7. Find. Jump to the next fermata, the next key change or the next time a given note appears.                           
8. Guitar players. Open a Guitar Pro or Ultimate Guitar tab and hear the strings, frets and sections read out.          
9. Importing MIDI. Turn a MIDI file into navigable parts.                                                               
10. The Mixer. Change instruments, volume and pan, and show that the lyrics part stays out of the way.                  
11. Playing along. Live MIDI input from your keyboard, and the performance indicator sounds.                            
12. Voice control. A few spoken commands, with Help > Voice Commands Reference as the place to learn more.              
13. Making it yours. Remapped shortcuts, recent files, and per-score settings being remembered.
